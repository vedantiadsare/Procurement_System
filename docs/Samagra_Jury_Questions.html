2The pitch in one minute

What is Samagra, in one sentence?

A single portal for sub-€50k sourcing at VW GDS India: requesters raise complete requests and collect supplier quotes, buyers compare them on one basis with AI help, and management sees turnaround time and workload as live numbers.

What problem does it solve?

  • Small purchases run on email and Excel. Requests arrive incomplete and bounce back and forth.
  • Quotes come in different formats, currencies and tax bases, so comparing them is manual and error-prone.
  • Nobody can see where a case stands without asking. TAT is argued about instead of measured.
  • There is no common record of why a supplier was chosen.
Say: "High-volume, low-value buying costs more effort per rupee than anything else Procurement does, and today it runs on email."

Why the name Samagra?

Samagra means "complete" or "holistic": the whole sourcing cycle in one place, from request to PO.

Who uses it?

  • Requesters: any employee who needs something bought.
  • GP buyers: check documents, compare offers, negotiate, release the PO.
  • Procurement head: portfolio dashboard by department, buyer, supplier, single sourcing and TAT.
  • Suppliers: no account; a private link to quote back.

What is the single biggest benefit?

Buyers spend their time negotiating instead of chasing documents and rebuilding spreadsheets. Negotiation is already the longest part of their work, and Samagra helps twice: it frees time for it, and the market price check gives them a reference price to negotiate with.

How to use these numbers

Don't sell Samagra as fixing TAT: GP already meets it 97–98% of the time. Sell effort, negotiation and visibility.

Any saving percentage is an assumption until a pilot measures it, so say "if" and "to be measured".

How does Samagra add business value?

  • Time: complete requests on first submission, automatic buyer assignment, automatic quote reading and ranking, status without phone calls.
  • Money: fair like-for-like comparison, and a market price check that gives buyers a number to negotiate with.
  • Control: TAT measured from the day documents are complete, single sourcing visible, full audit trail.
  • Compliance: InfoSec screening enforced before a request reaches Procurement.

Where exactly does it save time?

  • The form will not submit without the required documents: cart, SFO, RFQ, at least one offer, and InfoSec clearance where needed.
  • Each case goes straight to the buyer who owns its commodity.
  • Quotes are read, tax is stripped, currencies converted and offers ranked automatically.
  • The Rate Comparison workbook buyers already use is filled from the documents.
  • Each case shows its next action and alerts, so nobody has to work out what is pending.
  • Requesters are emailed at every step and can reply with attachments in the portal.

Does it save money, or only time?

Both. Comparing on the same basis prevents classic mistakes, such as a gross quote judged against a net one. The market price check shows when a supplier's price sits above what Amazon or Flipkart charge for the same model, which is direct negotiation leverage.

Say: "In the demo case, both suppliers were 4 to 9 percent above Flipkart on the monitor. That is a sentence a buyer can put in front of a supplier."

What is the ROI?

  • Time: 600 cases × 60–90 minutes = 600–900 buyer hours a year on these steps. If Samagra cuts that by 30–50%, that is 180–450 hours, or 22–56 working days of buyer time, freed for negotiation.
  • Rework: 60–90 requests a year bounce back today. The completeness check stops the missing-document share of those at submission.
  • Price: on ₹35–45 crore of annual spend, every 1% of better price is worth ₹35–45 lakh a year. The market price check applies to catalog products, so the real figure depends on that share of spend.
Say: "Six hundred cases, up to nine hundred buyer hours, thirty-five to forty-five crore of spend. Saving even a third of the hours and one percent of the price is material." Avoid: presenting 30–50% or 1% as measured. They are scenarios; the pilot measures the real figure.

Rework isn't even measured today. How would Samagra help?

Every "Ask requester" is recorded on the case, so Samagra measures rework automatically: how many requests go back, for what, and how long they wait. Today's 10–15% is an estimate; with Samagra it becomes a number on the dashboard.

Say: "You can't improve what you don't measure. Samagra measures it as a by-product."

How would you measure success after six months?

  • Average TAT per case, before and after
  • Percentage of cases closed within the TAT commitment (the dashboard already shows this)
  • Requests sent back for missing documents
  • Buyer hours per case
  • Savings negotiated using the market price check

How does GP benchmark prices today?

Against VW's own purchase history: for the same item, the buyer checks what was paid before and what was offered. That data stays inside VW, rightly. Samagra adds what history cannot show: what the same model sells for online today, at Amazon, Flipkart and other Indian retailers.

Say: "History tells you what you paid; the market tells you what it's worth today. A buyer negotiates best with both."

Why doesn't the demo use our purchase history?

Because it is VW data and must not leave VW. Running inside VW, Samagra would use it there: every case Samagra handles is stored with its items, every supplier's price, the winner and the date, so it builds the price history itself, and past SAP purchase orders could be imported once, inside VW, to start with years of history.

Avoid: implying the price-history view exists today. It is the next step on the roadmap; the online market check is what is built.

What does management gain?

Live visibility: open cases, TAT performance by month, workload by buyer, spend by department, single-sourcing share by department and supplier, all exportable to Excel. Today these numbers are assembled by hand, if at all.

How fast does the value show?

Immediately for visibility and complete requests, from the first week. Time savings in comparison and the price check build as cases flow through it.

4Impact on the VW organisation

How does it affect each group?

Requesters

  • Know exactly which documents are needed
  • See status without asking
  • Collect supplier quotes themselves through the portal

GP buyers

  • Complete cases arrive assigned to them
  • Offers already read and ranked
  • A market price to negotiate against

Procurement head

  • TAT, workload and single sourcing as live numbers
  • Evidence for every award decision

InfoSec

  • Every request screened against their five criteria
  • Nothing reaches Procurement without clearance where it is needed

Will it replace buyers?

No. It removes the clerical part of their job: checking completeness, reading quotes, building comparison sheets. Every decision (accept, ask, approve, reject, award) stays with a named person. Buyers get time back for negotiation and supplier management.

How does it support the sub-€50k rule?

Below roughly €50k the requester collects quotations themselves. Samagra gives them the tool to do that: an enquiry mailed to suppliers, private reply links, deadlines, quote versions, and an automatic comparison. Then they raise the sourcing request to Procurement.

How does it treat TAT fairly?

The clock starts on the date the document set is complete and correct, not when the request was first sent. A buyer who picks a case up late can backdate the start to when the documents were actually complete, so the requester is not penalised either. TAT is counted in working days: 15 up to ₹3.5 lakh, 30 up to ₹40 lakh.

5Users, adoption, change

Why would people switch from email?

Because it is less work for each of them. The requester gets status without asking, the form tells them exactly what is missing, suppliers reply through one link, and buyers stop rebuilding spreadsheets.

How much training is needed?

Very little. It is a guided form with plain-language questions, and the case page always says what to do next.

What if some requests still come by email?

The mailbox intake handles that. Samagra reads the procurement inbox, identifies the attachments (cart, SFO, RFQ, offers), proposes a case with each field's source, and a buyer confirms it. Nothing is created without a person confirming.

How would you roll it out?

  • Pilot with one or two departments and their buyers for a month.
  • Measure TAT, rework and buyer hours against the previous month.
  • Fix what the pilot shows, then extend department by department.

What about suppliers who are not tech-savvy?

They need nothing but email and a browser: no account, no password, no download. The enquiry and documents arrive by email, the link opens one simple page, and they can also simply reply to the email.

6Procurement process questions

What documents are required, and why does it differ?

One-time sourcing: approved Shopping Cart, Deviation Note/SFO, RFQ, at least one supplier offer. A frame contract has no cart yet (carts are raised per call-off), so it needs SFO, RFQ and offers, with Controlling's budget confirmation as an optional document. Any request where InfoSec criteria apply also needs clearance documents.

How are frame contracts handled differently?

Same documents and format as one-time sourcing except the cart, their own ticket series (FC- instead of SF-), and a frame contract number instead of a PO number at closure.

How is a case assigned to a buyer?

By expense commodity. The commodity list is split between the three buyers in configuration, and a case goes to its owner the moment it is submitted. A commodity nobody owns stays unclaimed in the queue for any buyer to pick up.

What happens above ₹40 lakh?

The case is marked out of scope for this portal and flagged to handle manually, because those purchases follow a different process.

How is currency handled?

The requester enters the value in the supplier's currency. The buyer sets the exchange rate per case, at the rate that applies when they work it. Until then a configured reference rate (₹94.5 per euro) is used only to estimate the TAT band, and is labelled as indicative.

How does InfoSec screening work?

  • When a requester floats an enquiry, a popup asks the five InfoSec questions. It is a heads-up, not a block, and suppliers are told they may need an NDA and TISAX or ISO 27001.
  • On the sourcing request, the same five questions are compulsory Yes/No. Any Yes makes clearance documents required, and the request cannot be submitted without them.
  • The buyer sees "Not needed", "Cleared" or "Clearance missing", with the reasons and the documents.

What is MRM, and how is it handled?

The requester states whether MRM is relevant and whether it was conducted. If conducted, the approval document is required; if not, a reason is required.

Can a requester answer a buyer's question without starting over?

Yes. The buyer's question appears on the requester's case page, and they reply there with text and attachments. The case returns to the buyer's queue.

7The AI

Where is AI used?

  • Reading quotations in any layout or language
  • Reading carts the table reader cannot parse
  • Turning an emailed request into case fields
  • Checking whether a quote covers what was asked
  • Commenting on terms and conditions
  • Drafting the covering note to suppliers
  • Answering questions about a case ("Ask this case")
  • Judging whether an online listing is a similar product

Why use AI at all? Couldn't rules do this?

The rules do the deciding. AI is used only for reading messy, unstructured documents, which rules cannot do reliably.

Say: "The model reads, the code decides, and a person makes every decision."

What if the AI gets a number wrong?

Every figure it reports is checked against the source document before it is shown. A price or model number that is not in the document is dropped, and anything uncertain is labelled. Ranking, tax and currency are calculated in code, not by the model.

What if the AI is down?

The deterministic half keeps working: forms, documents, TAT, assignment, dashboards, approvals. AI panels say plainly that the model is not available. At start-up, if LLMaaS cannot be reached, Samagra switches to the local model for that session.

Which model, and why?

gpt-4o through VW's LLMaaS, the approved enterprise service, for best quality. A local model through Ollama runs the same code offline and free. One setting switches between them.

Does the AI learn from our data? Is it trained on it?

No training and no fine-tuning. Pre-trained models only. Each call sends only the documents of the one case being worked on.

Can the AI make decisions on its own?

No. It drafts and recommends. Sending a clarification to a supplier, approving, rejecting and awarding are always a named person's click.

Are these real agents?

Three small agents with a logged perceive-decide-act cycle: the Offer agent wakes when a quotation lands and ranks offers; the Enquiry agent drafts supplier notes; the Advisor is pure rules and recommends the next action. Every run is recorded for audit.

Avoid: overselling "autonomous agents". They are bounded helpers, and a person approves every outward action.

8Functional vs mocked

They will ask this directly. Say it before they ask.

Real and working live

  • AI reading, offer comparison, Ask this case (gpt-4o or local)
  • Email through Microsoft Graph, landing in real inboxes
  • Supplier portal with private links over a public tunnel, quote versions
  • Market price check against live Amazon.in, Flipkart and Google Shopping
  • Working-day TAT, buyer allocation, InfoSec screening, alerts and next action
  • Dashboards and Excel exports, Rate Comparison workbook
  • Email intake from the procurement mailbox

Simulated or not built

  • Case data is synthetic: 40 demo cases with generated documents
  • Exchange rates are configured reference rates, not live feeds
  • No SAP integration: the buyer types the PO number
  • No VW single sign-on: demo accounts with passwords
  • Some demo users' email addresses are not real mailboxes
  • Runs on a laptop, not VW hosting

Is the demo data real VW data?

No. It is synthetic, built in the style of the challenge dataset: realistic suppliers, values and documents, but invented. No real VW procurement data is in the system.

10Scaling beyond VW GDS India

Can other parts of VW use it?

Yes. Any VW Group unit with an indirect procurement team faces the same small-spend problem. What is specific to GDS India (the commodity-to-buyer map, TAT bands, required documents, InfoSec questions, departments) is configuration, not code.

What would change for another country or company?

  • Configuration: departments, commodities, buyers, thresholds, currency.
  • Market price check: point the search at that country's retailers.
  • Tax: GST today; VAT handling already exists for European quotes.
  • Language: the AI already reads quotations in German, Italian and other languages.

Could it be sold outside VW?

This kind of buying is often called tail spend: many small purchases with high admin cost. Every large company has it, and large procurement suites rarely handle it lightly. Samagra could be offered as a lightweight front end for tail spend, to mid-size enterprises and shared-service centres.

Who would pay for it, and how?

Internally: a Procurement or IT cost centre, justified by buyer hours and savings. Externally: a subscription per buyer seat or per volume of requests. Present this as an option, not a plan; the first goal is internal use.

Would it handle many more users and cases?

The architecture already separates the web layer, business logic and data. For VW-wide use, SQLite moves to Postgres, the server runs as several workers on VW cloud, and files move to object storage. The code does not need redesigning.

11Competition and differentiation

Isn't this what SAP Ariba or Coupa already do?

Those are full procurement suites built for strategic sourcing, with long implementations and per-user licences. Samagra targets the small-spend tail that those suites handle heavily or not at all, and it is designed to feed SAP rather than replace it.

Why build instead of buy?

  • It fits VW GDS's exact process: the SFO, the TAT bands, the commodity split, the InfoSec criteria.
  • It runs on VW's approved AI and keeps data in VW systems.
  • It is small enough to own and change in-house.

What makes Samagra different?

  • Every AI figure verified against the source document.
  • Market price check that refuses to compare unlike products.
  • A fair TAT clock that starts when documents are complete.
  • Supplier portal without accounts, with quote versions.
  • InfoSec built into the request itself.

Why not just an Excel template or Power Automate flow?

Excel cannot read a quotation, enforce completeness, give suppliers a private link, or show a live dashboard across all cases. A flow could route emails, but not compare quotes on one basis or check online prices.

12Security, privacy, compliance

How is access controlled?

Every page declares which role may open it, and the check runs before the page's code. Requesters see only their own cases, buyers see the queue, the head sees the portfolio. Sessions are signed cookies, and the role is re-checked on every request.

How are supplier links secured?

Each supplier gets a random 256-bit token. It contains no information, opens exactly one invitation, stops working at the deadline, and can be revoked by closing the enquiry. No supplier can see another's response.

Where does company data go?

Documents and data stay on the Samagra server. The AI runs on VW's LLMaaS or entirely locally. Only one case's documents are sent per AI call. The price search sends product names only, never prices or supplier names.

How are passwords and secrets handled?

Passwords are stored as salted PBKDF2-SHA256 hashes. API keys and credentials are environment variables, never in code or in the repository.

What about personal data and data protection law?

Samagra stores names and work emails of employees and supplier contacts, which is what procurement needs. In production it would follow VW's data protection rules and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act: hosting on VW infrastructure, access by role, and retention rules for closed cases.

Is there an audit trail?

Yes. Every action on a case is recorded with who did it and when, marked as visible to the requester or internal to Procurement. Every agent run is logged. The "Ready to award?" tab assembles the evidence behind a decision.

13Cost, hosting, integration

What does it cost to run?

  • Hosting: one small server or container. It runs today on a laptop.
  • AI: a handful of model calls per case on LLMaaS, metered; the local model costs nothing. The whole jury demo runs inside a $5 LLMaaS allowance.
  • Price search: SerpAPI, about 9 searches per market check on a three-item case; paid plans scale with volume.
  • Email: Microsoft Graph, part of existing Microsoft licensing.
Avoid: giving a cost per case you have not measured. Say it is small and metered, and offer to measure it in the pilot.

What is needed to go live?

  • VW single sign-on instead of demo accounts
  • Postgres instead of SQLite
  • SAP integration: carts in, PO numbers out
  • Hosting on VW cloud or an internal server

All four are on the architecture slide as proposed.

How would SAP integration work?

Read the approved cart from SAP by SC number instead of uploading a PDF, and write the PO number back when the buyer approves. Samagra already holds both fields, so the integration replaces manual steps without changing the flow.

Who maintains it?

It is plain Python with standard libraries and documented code, small enough for one internal developer to maintain. Business rules live in one configuration file.

14Risks and limitations

What are the weaknesses today?

  • Not yet tested on real VW documents at volume; the AI needs tuning on them.
  • No SAP or SSO integration yet.
  • InfoSec clearance is per request; remembering cleared suppliers is a next step.
  • The market price check only works for catalog products with online prices.

What if online prices are wrong or misleading?

The check only claims "same product" when the model number matches, drops accessories, used items and import stores, ignores prices far from the rest, prefers major retailers, and shows the source and date of every price. It is presented as a negotiation reference, not proof of overcharging.

What if users don't adopt it?

Start with a pilot where the benefit is obvious, measure it, and let the dashboard make the case. Requests by email are still accepted through the mailbox intake, so adoption can be gradual.

What if the AI provider changes or becomes expensive?

All AI calls go through one file and an OpenAI-compatible interface. Switching provider or model is configuration, and a local model is already supported.

16Tough and curveball questions

Why should we pick your solution over the others?

It covers the whole cycle end to end and works live: request, supplier quotes, comparison, market price, InfoSec, approval and dashboards. Every AI output is verified, and it is built around VW GDS's actual process and documents.

What did you build versus reuse?

The application, workflows, comparison logic, verification rules, agents, supplier portal and dashboards were built by the team. We use standard open-source libraries (FastAPI, pdfplumber, openpyxl, Chart.js) and external services (VW LLMaaS, Microsoft Graph, SerpAPI). No starter code.

TAT is already met 97–98% of the time. Why do we need this?

Agreed: GP performs well on TAT. Samagra is not about rescuing TAT. It is about the 600–900 buyer hours a year spent on paperwork around it, the 10–15% of requests that bounce back, and giving buyers more time and better information for negotiation, the slowest part of their work. It also keeps TAT performance visible without manual reporting.

Avoid: any claim that TAT is a problem today. It would contradict the challenge owner in front of the jury.

What would you cut if you had to?

The dashboards' extra views and the email intake, before anything in the core cycle: complete request, comparison, approval with a fair TAT.

What was the hardest problem?

Making AI output trustworthy: verifying every figure against the source document, and making the market price check refuse wrong products, since a single cheap accessory or refurbished listing would otherwise wreck the comparison.

What would you do differently?

Start from the real VW document formats and process rules earlier. Several features (InfoSec, the sub-€50k flow, frame contracts) were reshaped after feedback from Procurement.

Does it work without internet?

The core works offline with the local AI model. Email, supplier links and the market price check need internet by nature.

How do you know the comparison is right?

Each step is shown: the figure as quoted, whether tax was included and at what rate, the conversion, and the comparable value. A buyer can check every number against the document, which is one click away.

Is this just a demo, or could it run tomorrow?

The workflow, AI, email and supplier portal run live today. Running it for real needs VW hosting, SSO and, ideally, SAP integration, which is integration work rather than new product work.
