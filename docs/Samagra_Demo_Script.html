3How to explain the flow in one breath

Under fifty thousand euros the requester runs the supplier enquiry, collects the quotes, then hands Procurement a complete request. Samagra covers that whole path: the enquiry, the quotes, the request, the buyer's comparison and negotiation, and the PO. The AI only reads documents; the rules decide; a person approves every step.

4The scenes

A · Float an enquiry (requester)

Click
Vedanti → Supplier Quotes → Float a new enquiry → in step 2 attach the RFQ PDF.
You'll see
"Reading RFQ_….pdf and drafting the note". A few seconds later Subject, the note and Respond by are filled in.
Say

The requester only attaches the RFQ. The Enquiry agent reads it and writes the supplier mail: subject, quantities, specs, what the quote must state. Every quantity it writes is checked against the RFQ; anything it can't find becomes "to confirm". She reads it, edits if needed, and she sends it, not the AI.

Then
Add the supplier email (yours, or a juror's), press Send enquiry. The InfoSec popup appears: answer it.
Say

Before anything goes out, the requester answers InfoSec's five screening questions. If a supplier will touch VW data or systems, they are told up front that an NDA and TISAX may be needed.

B · The supplier replies (phone)

Click
On the phone, open the mail → the private link → Yes → attach a quotation → Submit quotation.
Say

The supplier has no account and no password: a private link, valid until the deadline, that shows only their own enquiry. They can quote, send a revision, or decline, from a phone.

If a juror offers their email: use it. It's the most convincing moment of the demo. If the network is slow, move on to the prepared set and come back.

C · Versions and the automatic comparison (requester)

Click
Open the prepared enquiry (e.g. headsets).
You'll see
Each supplier with "Quotation · 4 versions", older ones greyed; Soundline "Declined"; the comparison at the top ranking each supplier's latest version.
Say

Nexgen revised four times; nothing is overwritten, every version is kept for negotiation. The moment a quote lands, the Offer agent reads it and ranks the latest version of each supplier, net of GST. Prism's second version was priced including GST. Whenever a quote includes tax, Samagra spots it and strips it before ranking, so the comparison stays like for like.

D · The sourcing request (requester)

Click
Open the submitted request's page; optionally show the new-request form and try to submit without one document.
Say

The form won't submit without the cart, SFO, RFQ, at least one offer, and InfoSec clearance when any screening answer is Yes. Incomplete requests never reach a buyer, and that's where today's 10 to 15 percent rework comes from.

E · The buyer

Click
Vikas → Queue → the headsets case.
You'll see
The Next action box at the top with its reasons; the TAT clock on the right; tabs Summary, Documents, Activity, Ready to award?
Say

The case went straight to Vikas because he owns headsets. The box at the top is the Advisor: it reads the case's facts and says the one next thing to do, with the reasons. Pure rules, no AI, so it's the same every time. The TAT clock counts working days from the day the documents were complete.

5Explaining Analyze offers

Click
Summary tab → AI offer analysis → Analyze offers with AI (or Re-analyze offers).
You'll see
A table per supplier: the total as quoted (Native), whether tax was included, the comparable value (Normalised), the rank, and a confidence; terms like payment, validity, delivery; anything missing from a quote; and Export rate sheet.
What it does
  1. The AI reads each quotation, whatever its layout: total, currency, whether GST is included, terms.
  2. Code checks every figure appears in the document. If it doesn't, it isn't used.
  3. Code strips GST, converts currency into the case's currency, and ranks.
  4. It flags gaps (for example "tax basis not stated") and fills the Rate Comparison workbook buyers already use.
Say

This replaces rebuilding the comparison sheet by hand. The AI reads each quote; the code does the arithmetic. It strips GST, converts currency and ranks, so a gross quote is never compared against a net one. The model is never asked which supplier is cheapest. Here Nexgen is first at 2,72,500 against Prism's 2,75,300, one percent apart. The buyer can export this straight into the Rate Comparison sheet.

If asked "what if the AI reads a number wrong?": every figure is checked against the document before it's used, and the confidence and the source document are shown next to each row.

6Explaining the market price check

Click
Summary tab → Market price check → Check online prices (or Check again).
You'll see
Per product, one of three verdicts:
  • "Same product on Amazon.in at ₹…" (plus "cheapest major retailer"): the exact model was found;
  • "No exact match. Comparable products typically sell for ₹… – ₹…": an indicative band;
  • "…too few for a reliable price band, so no verdict is given": not enough evidence.
Under each, every supplier's price with its gap to the market, and the listings with links. Installation and other service lines are listed as not price-checked.
What it does
  1. Searches Google Shopping India and Amazon.in for each catalogue product.
  2. Keeps trusted retailers only (Amazon, Flipkart, Croma, Reliance Digital…), and drops used, refurbished, imported, accessories and spare parts.
  3. The model number must match exactly and the stated specs (RAM, storage, VA) must be present. The AI judges "same product?", and code makes the final call.
  4. Puts the supplier's price on the same footing as the shop price (GST included) and shows the gap.
Say

Today a buyer benchmarks against VW's purchase history. That tells you what you paid. This tells you what the same model costs today at Amazon and Flipkart. It's a reference for negotiation, not a rule: a retail price has no enterprise warranty or delivery to site, but a buyer can now say "your price is so many percent above Flipkart, explain the gap." If prices improve one percent on GP's 35 to 45 crore, that's 35 to 45 lakh a year, and that's what a pilot would measure.

If a product shows "no match": say it plainly. "It only shows a price when it's sure it's the same model, from a trusted shop. Here it isn't, so it won't guess." That is a strength, not a failure.

Finish the case

Optional
Ask this case (right side): "What delivery time did Nexgen offer?" It answers from the documents and names the source. Ask something not in them, and it says so.
Click
Approve with a PO number; then try the same PO on another case and show the refusal.
Say

The PO number closes the case and the requester is emailed. One PO, one case: the database refuses a duplicate, and every step is in the activity trail, so "why did Nexgen win?" is answerable months later.

Honest note: the demo data is synthetic, so the numbers illustrate the view; the pilot would fill it with real cases.
